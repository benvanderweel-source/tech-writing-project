# How to Process a Customer Payment Over the Phone

## Purpose and Scope

**Purpose:** This guide walks you through processing a customer payment by phone — from greeting the caller to closing the call — accurately, securely, and in compliance with call-recording and PCI DSS payment regulations.
**Scope:** This guide covers credit card payments made over the phone only. It does not cover payments made through the web portal, in a branch, or by mail, nor does it address refunds, disputes, or chargebacks.
**Audience:** New customer service representatives taking live payment calls, as well as experienced representatives who want a refresher.
**Before you begin:** You should be logged into your account management system and ready to take calls.

---

## Greeting

1. Introduce yourself by first name only.
2. State the name of your employer.
3. Ask the purpose of the call.
4. Speak clearly and calmly. This helps assure the customer that you are competent and ready to assist them.

> EXAMPLE: "Hello, this is Adam at Stone Bank. How can I help you today?"

---

## Recording Disclosure

1. Ask the caller for their name.
2. Thank them for their call, using their name.
3. Inform the customer that the call is being recorded and explain the reason. 


**NOTE:** Stating the disclosure at the outset of the call:

  * Ensures transparency and helps build trust.
  * Helps you comply with state and federal regulations regarding consent to record a phone conversation.
  * Protects your employer from potential legal issues if a caller later raises a complaint about something that happened during the call.
  * Informs the caller that the recording may be reviewed for quality assurance and training purposes.

> EXAMPLE: "Thank you for your call, Mr. Williams. I just need to inform you that this call will be recorded for quality and training purposes."

### If the Customer Declines to Be Recorded

1. Politely explain that you cannot proceed without recording the call.
2. Empathize with their objection.
3. Offer alternative payment options.
4. If they still decline, thank them for their call and wish them a good day before disconnecting.

> EXAMPLE: "I apologize, Mr. Williams. I understand that you don't want to be recorded. Unfortunately, I'll be unable to proceed without the call being recorded. You can always make payments through our web portal, at your local branch, or via mail. Thank you for calling Stone Bank, and have a good day."

### If the Customer Agrees to Be Recorded

1. Thank them for their understanding and cooperation.
2. Proceed to the next step and access their account.

> EXAMPLE: "Thank you for your understanding, Mr. Williams. I appreciate your cooperation, and I'm here to help you with your payment today."

---

## Accessing the Account

Enter one of the following into Stone Bank's account management system to locate the customer's account:

* Account number
* Phone number
* Date of birth

### If No Account Is Found
1. Ask the customer to confirm the information they provided and try again.
2. If searching by phone number returns no results, ask the customer if the account might be registered under a different phone number and try again. If still not found, attempt lookup by account number or date of birth before concluding the account cannot be located.
3. If the account still cannot be located, inform the customer and offer alternative payment options (web portal, branch, or mail).

**NOTE:** It's helpful to ask the customer if the phone number could be a previous number, a spouse's number, or a number they no longer use.

### If Multiple Accounts Are Found
1. Ask if the customer has multiple accounts and ask them to confirm the account number for the account they wish to make a payment on.
2. If the customer does not have multiple accounts, ask them to confirm the last four digits of their Social Security number.

---

## Verifying the Caller

Collect any three of the following to verify the caller:

* Account number
* Phone number
* Date of birth
* Last four digits of the Social Security number
* Address
* **Exact** amount of the last payment

1. Count any information already collected earlier in the call toward verification. Do not ask for it again, as this may make the customer think you were not paying attention.
2. If the caller's name differs from the account holder's name, check for authorized users on the account.
3. If the caller is the account holder or an authorized user, continue to the next step.
4. If the caller is not an authorized user, Stone Bank's company policy will still allow you to accept a payment. However, you may not disclose account information such as balances, payment history, or prior transactions.

### If You Cannot Verify the Caller

1. Politely explain that you are unable to verify their identity and therefore cannot assist them with a payment over the phone.
2. Empathize with their situation.
3. If they ask why, explain that these security measures protect the account from unauthorized access and fraud.
4. Reassure them that these measures exist for their protection.
5. Offer alternative payment options, such as:

  * Web portal
  * Local branch
  * Mail

6. Thank them for their call and wish them a good day before disconnecting.

---

## Determining the Payment Method

Stone Bank allows for two types of payments over the phone. 
Ask which one the customer would like to use, and proceed accordingly:

  * ACH payments
  * Debit card payments


### ACH Payments (Automated Clearing House)

1. Ask for the 9-digit routing number.
2. Repeat the routing number to confirm it.
3. The system will display the associated bank name; confirm that information as well.
4. Ask for the bank account number.
5. Confirm the account number by reading it back.

### Debit Card Payments

Stone Bank's call recording system pauses recording during card collection. Representatives may read back card details **only at the time you collect them, and only to confirm accuracy.**

If a customer begins reading card digits before you are ready: Ask them to pause, confirm you are ready to capture the information, then ask them to begin again.

If the customer has already used a debit card for payments, you will only be able to see the last four digits of the card number. Confirm the last four digits of the card number with the customer and ask whether they want to use the same card or a different one.

> EXAMPLE: "Mr. Williams, I see that your last payment was made with a debit card ending in 5678. Would you like to use the same card for this payment, or would you like to use a different card?"

* If they want to use the same card, you will only need to collect and confirm the three- or four-digit CVV number. It's located on the back of most cards, or the front on some — and note that Stone Bank's system does not store it, so you must collect it for each payment.
  
  
* If they want to use a different card:
  1. Ask for the cardholder's name as it appears on the card.
  2. Ask for the full 16-digit debit card number, and read it back to confirm accuracy. 
  3. Ask for the card's expiration date, and read it back to confirm accuracy.
  4. Ask for the three- or four-digit CVV number, and read it back to confirm accuracy.

---

## Taking the Payment

1. Ask the customer how much they would like to pay.
2. Confirm the amount with the customer.

### If the Customer Wants to Pay Less Than the Minimum Payment

1. Inform them of the minimum payment amount.
2. Ask whether they would like to pay that amount instead.
3. If they still want to pay less than the minimum payment, process the payment as requested after confirming they understand the remaining balance is still due by the due date to avoid late fees.


### If the Customer Wants to Pay More Than the Total Balance

1. Inform them of the total balance, and inform them that the system cannot accept a payment exceeding the balance.

> EXAMPLE: "Mr. Williams, your total balance is $150.00. The system won't accept a payment higher than the balance, but I can take any amount up to that."

2. Ask how much they would like to pay.
3. If they confirm, proceed with the lower amount as you would any standard payment.
4. If they decline, no payment can be processed, and you should offer alternative payment options, such as:

  * Web portal
  * Local branch
  * Mail

---

## Payment Confirmation

Stone Bank's payment confirmation process follows a specific protocol. 

The confirmation must include:

1. Repeating the customer's name.
2. Giving the payment date.
3. Giving the payment amount.
4. Confirming the last four digits of the customer's account number.
5. Confirming the payment method and the last four digits of the bank account or debit card.
6. Asking the customer to confirm the information and authorize the payment.

> EXAMPLE: "Just to confirm, Mr. Williams, you're requesting on June 1st, 2026, to make a payment of $150.00 to your account ending in 1234, using your debit card ending in 5678. Is that correct? Would you like to proceed with this payment?"

   This recap serves two purposes:
   * It allows the customer to catch mistakes before the payment is processed, helping prevent declines or payments being applied incorrectly.
   * It protects you and your employer if a customer later disputes the payment or claims they did not authorize it.

7. If the customer confirms the information, process the payment.
8. If not, identify and correct any errors before proceeding.

---

## Processing the Payment

After confirming the payment details:

1. Enter the payment information into the system.
2. Submit the payment.
3. Wait for confirmation.

### If the Payment Is Declined

1. Inform the customer.
2. Confirm the payment information with the customer.
3. Attempt the payment a second time. You must collect and confirm the CVV number again if the payment method is a debit card.
4. If the payment is declined again, advise the customer to use another payment method or contact their bank.

### If the Payment Is Successful

1. Inform the customer that the payment has been received.
2. Explain when the payment will be applied to the account, usually within one to three business days.
3. Proactively offer to provide a confirmation number. If they do not want one, proceed to closing the call.

---

## Closing the Call

1. Ask whether there is anything else you can help them with. This demonstrates attentiveness and a willingness to assist with additional concerns.
2. If the customer has no further questions, thank them for their payment and for calling.
3. Use their name to personalize the interaction.

> EXAMPLE: "Thank you for your payment, Mr. Williams. We appreciate your business and thank you for calling Stone Bank. Have a great day!"

4. Allow the customer to disconnect first.
5. After the customer hangs up, disconnect the call on your end.

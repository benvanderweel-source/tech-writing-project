# FIVE TECHNIQUES FOR DIFFICULT CALLS

*Prepared by Ben Vanderweel | 7/29/26*  
*A Quick Reference for Welbe Advocates*

**Remember: These are behaviors, not personality traits.**

*You don't need to feel calm, confident, or patient right away. You only need to practice the behaviors.*

### THE GOAL
*Sound like someone who heard them and is already doing something about it.*

---

## 1. BE A PERSON IN MOTION
**Narrate your actions.**

Instead of leaving silence, tell the caller what you're doing. Use language that puts you in motion. The caller should be able to picture you doing something.

*Examples*
- "Let me get on top of that for you."
- "I'm pulling up their transportation record right now."
- "Give me one minute. I'm going to shake loose some answers."
- "I'm sending this to the team that has those answers."

**Why it works:**  
People feel more comfortable waiting when they know what's happening. Narrating your actions and telling them what you're going to do helps reassure them that progress is being made.

---

## 2. PROVE YOU HEARD THEM
**Show understanding with specifics.**

Avoid generic responses like "I understand."

*Examples*
- "So, she's been waiting at Labcorp since one o'clock and nobody's come?"
- "You're saying you've called twice already about this appointment and still haven't heard back."
- "The driver went to the old address again, even after you called to change it?"

**Why it works:**  
Repeating details proves you're listening and validates the caller's experience.

---

## 3. MATCH THE CALLER
**Meet them where they are first.**

| Caller State | Your Approach |
|---|---|
| Urgent | Increase pace, use shorter sentences |
| Distressed | Slow down, soften your tone |
| Frustrated | Acknowledge concerns, stay steady |

**Why it works:**  
You can't lead someone from a place they aren't standing. Match first, then guide.

---

## 4. SOUND CONFIDENT
**Confidence comes from technique.**

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "I'll find out." | "I think maybe..." |
| "Here's what I'm going to do." | "Kind of..." |
| Speak in statements. | "Probably..." |
| Apologize once, then move forward | Repeated apologies |

**Why it works:**  
Callers often take their emotional cue from your voice. Confidence creates stability. It's not about knowing everything — it's about not sounding lost while you find out.

---

## 5. SMILE ON CALLS
**Yes, they can hear it.**

Smile during your greeting, not after the situation becomes difficult.

*Benefits*
- It creates a warmer, friendlier tone.
- It helps lower your own stress response.
- It makes conversations feel more personal.
- It signals to the caller that you're genuinely willing to help.

**Why it works:**  
Warmth is audible. So is its absence.

---

### QUICK REFERENCE
*Remember:*
- [ ] Tell them what you're doing
- [ ] Prove you heard them
- [ ] Match their pace and tone
- [ ] Speak confidently
- [ ] Smile before you answer
